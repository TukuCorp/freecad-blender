## 3. Findings — Topic 2: Fire and smoke

### S-06

- **ID:** S-06
- **Severity:** CRITICAL
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, "Common to all nine sheets": *"No designer, date, revision, stamp or signature is visible on any of these nine photos."*; and *"PHẦN GHI CHÚ" (remarks) box is blank on every sheet.* Structural sheets, `transcript-foundation-stairs.md`: *"Title-block scale box reads 1/20 on every sheet"*, and `transcript-beams-slabs.md`: *"No designer name, engineer stamp/licence number, date or revision table is visible (title block is cropped at the right/bottom edge in all photos, so absence cannot be confirmed - PARTIAL)."*
- **Issue:** **No fire-safety information exists anywhere in the set.** There is no fire note, no compartmentation note, no smoke-control note, no alarm note, no stair-enclosure note, no fire-door schedule, and no ceiling height on any of the 9 architectural sheets. The "PHẦN GHI CHÚ" (remarks) box is blank on every sheet. No sheet carries a designer name, stamp, date or revision table (the structural title blocks are cropped, so their absence is **PARTIAL**, but the architectural ones are fully visible and definitively blank).
- **Why it matters:** Every fire-safety finding below exists because the drawing set is silent. A set that carries no fire information cannot be permit-ready, cannot be built against, and cannot be defended in an insurance or liability dispute. It also means the contractor is building to an unspecified fire strategy — the drawings do not tell the site what to build.

SCDF clause **2.2.13(b)** requires a 1-hour separation wall and a half-hour self-closing exit access door.
- **Basis class:** LOCAL-MINIMUM
- **Fix:** Add a **fire-safety sheet** (a fire-strategy sheet; the Vietnamese title the owner's authority will expect is "PHƯƠNG ÁN AN TOÀN CHÁY", which is a name this review proposes, not one read from the drawings) to the set, referenced from every plan, carrying: fire-resistance grade of the house (bậc chịu lửa) and the resulting Table 4 fire resistances; the compartmentation line; the stair enclosure type (L1/N1/L2/N3/L1-protected), door type and rating at every level; the smoke-control provision; the alarm and detection layout; the sprinkler/mist decision; the fire-lift decision; and the ventilation openings and their fire ratings. Until this sheet exists, every other fire finding in this report is provisional.
- **Who decides:** architect (authoring) with the fire consultant (content), and the designer must sign and stamp it.
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (transcript lines quoted; clauses read from the fetched documents). The structural sheets' title blocks are cropped in the photographs, so the absence of an engineer stamp on **them** is **PARTIAL** — see Question 26 and S-43.

### S-07

- **ID:** S-07
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, "Common to all nine sheets": *"Door/window/finish tag circles recur: D1 (front door), Dc (front gate), D2 (rear door), D3 (loggia door), D4 (internal), D5 (balcony door), D6, D7 (5th floor), Dw (WC door), W1, K1/K2 (glass wall/window), S1/S2 (windows), Sb1 (WC/light-well window) … No door/window schedule is in this set of nine photos."*
- **Issue:** The set contains **no door schedule and no fire-door specification**. Tags D1–D7, Dw, W1, K1/K2, S1/S2, Sb1 are used freely, including "D4 (internal)" and "Dw (WC door)" which are explicitly internal doors, but the set never says whether **any** door is a fire door, whether the stair has a door at all, or what the door to the stairwell is made of.
- **Why it matters:** A fire door is the single cheapest life-safety measure in this whole report and the cheapest to specify. On a single-stair house, an E 20 self-closing door at each stair entrance converts a smoke-filled stair into a survivable stair for the cost of a door leaf. Without a schedule, the site will install ordinary doors.
- **Basis:** UK ADB **para. 2.5(a)** (protected stairway, REI 30 at all storeys) and **para. 2.6(a)(ii)** (*"a landing within the protected stairway enclosure to an alternative escape route on the same storey"*, separated by REI 30 and a fire door per Diagram 2.4). QCVN 06 clause **3.4.7** — stairwells must connect *"qua tiền sảnh được ngăn cách … bằng các vách ngăn cháy loại 1 có cửa đi với cơ cấu tự đóng và khe cửa phải được chèn kín"*. SCDF clause **2.2.13(b)** — *"The exit access door shall have fire resistance of half an hour fitted with automatic self closing device."*
- **Basis class:** LOCAL-MINIMUM (QCVN 06 3.4.7) and BEYOND-LOCAL (ADB, SCDF)
- **Fix:** Issue a door schedule. Minimum content: for every door tag — leaf material, fire rating (EI 30 / E 20 / none), self-closing device (type and rating), intumescent/smoke seals, glazed panel (if any) rating, swing direction (must not swing into the escape route), and the room pair. The doors into the stair enclosure on every level must be self-closing fire doors. The same schedule must cover the WC doors (Dw), the kitchen-to-living separation, and the garage-to-house door (S-09).
- **Who decides:** architect (schedule), fire consultant (ratings), contractor (supply).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (absence of the schedule from the transcript; clauses read from the fetched documents).

### S-08

- **ID:** S-08
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, IMG_9972 "MẶT BẰNG TẦNG 1": room chain *"P. Khách (living, 3800 / 4000) -> stair … -> P. Ăn + bếp (dining + kitchen, 4400 / 4500; sink, hob and fridge drawn) -> WC (tags W1, Sb1, Dw, door D2 rear) -> Sân sau (-0.200)"*.
- **Issue:** The **kitchen with hob and fridge sits at the rear of the ground floor, immediately above the rear yard** and with the rear WC directly beside it — the classic single-family-house kitchen-at-the-rear arrangement. There is no fire separation between the kitchen and the living room (door D4/D5 type is undocumented), no range hood duct detail, no gas or LPG information at all, and the kitchen is 4.4–4.5 m from the only stair. **The one fire load that reliably starts house fires (cooking oil) is directly in the escape path of the ground floor.**
- **Why it matters:** A grease fire in the rear kitchen puts smoke in the ground-floor corridor between the kitchen and the front door. Because the stair is the only route and the kitchen is at the far end of it, the occupants have to pass the fire. There is no second exit from the rear yard at ground level to step into — D2 leads to a rear yard that is enclosed by a 2600 mm boundary element, which may not be a usable final exit at all.
- **Basis:** UK ADB **para. 2.11(a)** permits a kitchen as an *"inner room"* (a room you pass through to reach another room) **only** in a house with no other inner room on the same route; ADB **para. 2.12** — a room accessed only via an inner room requires *"the access rooms each have a smoke alarm"* and *"None of the access rooms is a kitchen."* QCVN 06 clause **3.2.13(e)** and the general requirement that a kitchen be a separated space. SCDF Table 2.2A gives the kitchen's occupant load.
- **Basis class:** BEYOND-LOCAL (ADB 2.11–2.12) — the point is that the *international* practice treats a rear kitchen in a single-stair house as a life-safety issue, not a comfort issue.
- **Fix:** (i) Fit a fire-rated separation between the kitchen and the rest of the ground floor — a self-closing E 30 door plus a rated wall — so that a kitchen fire is contained and the escape path to the front door stays clear; (ii) give the kitchen a mechanical extract hood discharging to the roof (never into the light well or the stair), plus a heat alarm over the hob; (iii) keep a fire extinguisher (or a domestic mist/extinguisher system) at the kitchen threshold; (iv) establish what the 2600 mm rear boundary element actually is — a fence, a wall or a gate — and make D2 discharge to a space a person can be rescued from. The rear yard is only 2.3–2.5 m deep, so a solid 2600 mm boundary would make D2 a trap rather than an exit.
- **Who decides:** architect (re-plan/separation), fire consultant (rating and detection), owner (hood, alarm, extinguisher).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (kitchen position and fittings from the transcript; ADB 2.11/2.12 read from the fetched document). The absence of any extraction or separation detail is VERIFIED as an absence from the set; what exists on site is **NOT FOUND** — unknown and not ascertainable from the drawings.

### S-09

- **ID:** S-09
- **Severity:** CRITICAL
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, IMG_9972: *"Nơi để xe (parking) +-0.000, tag N1, door D1"* — the parking bay at ±0.000 is the **front** zone under the double-height mezzanine void (IMG_9973: *"the front yard void (3900 wide) and the interior front void (width 2800, tag K1 on the glass wall)"*).
- **Issue:** A **car (and by implication EV charging) is parked inside the house**, directly under the double-height void and under the habitable mezzanine ("P. Sinh hoạt" at +3.800) and the front bedroom above that. The set contains **no separation of any kind** between the parking bay and the habitable floors above: no fire-rated slab, no ceiling, no firewall, no ventilation opening, no EV charging provision, no note of any kind. The garage is not a basement — it is a room in the house with a car in it.
- **Why it matters:** Three separate failure scenarios, any one of which is fatal:
  1. **Vehicle fire in the bay.** A car is fuel, tyres, plastics and a lithium battery. With no separation, the fire products pass straight into the double-height void and up into the living room and bedroom. This is the ADB paragraph 5.6 scenario exactly.
  2. **EV charging in the bay.** Charging a lithium-ion pack inside a narrow concrete bay with no ventilation, no detection and no isolation is the single most common cause of lithium-ion fires in residential buildings worldwide. A thermal runaway cell is not extinguishable by the occupants.
  3. **Smoke, not fire.** Even a minor fire or a burnt battery produces enough smoke to make the double-height void, the front balcony glazing and the front door unusable — which removes the front exit from the escape strategy for the whole house.
- **Basis:** UK ADB **para. 5.6** — *"If a garage is attached to or forms an integral part of a dwellinghouse, the garage should be separated from the rest of the dwellinghouse by fire resisting construction (minimum REI 30) (Diagram 5.1)."* ADB **para. 5.7** and Diagram 5.1 — the door between dwellinghouse and garage must be *"a minimum of 100mm above the level of the garage floor"* or the floor must fall away from the door, and the diagram requires *"Fire doors between the garage and dwellinghouse to have a minimum fire resistance of E 30 Sa and be fitted with a self-closing device"* with *"Wall and any floor between the garage and dwellinghouse to have a minimum fire resistance of REI 30 from the garage side."* QCVN 06 clause **3.2.6.2(a)** — a single exit is permitted only if *"Không được để xe cơ giới, không được bố trí kho hạng A, B, C tại tầng có lối thoát nạn ra ngoài nhà, trừ khi các khu vực này được ngăn cách theo các quy định của quy chuẩn này"* — i.e. **no motor vehicles on the exit storey unless separated in accordance with the quy chuẩn**. QCVN 06 clause 1.1.2 note 1 requires the garage-specific standard to be applied additionally for basement car parks; this is not a basement but the principle is the same.
- **Basis class:** BEYOND-LOCAL (ADB 5.6–5.7) and LOCAL-MINIMUM (QCVN 06 3.2.6.2(a))
- **Fix:** Make the parking bay a **properly separated garage**: a REI 30 floor slab (or an equivalent rated ceiling assembly) between the bay and the void, a REI 30 separation from the rest of the ground floor with an E 30 Sa self-closing fire door, a 100 mm step up from the bay floor into the house (or a fall away from the door), natural cross-ventilation to outside (not into the void), and a smoke/heat detection linked to the house alarm. **If EV charging is to be provided, it must be outdoors or in a separated, ventilated, non-habitable enclosure with its own detection and isolation — never in this bay.** If the owner keeps the bay as an open covered parking space with no car, S-09 reduces to a ventilation and guarding issue.
- **Who decides:** architect (re-plan of the ground floor), fire consultant (the separation detail), owner (whether to keep the car in the house at all — this is the pivotal decision), contractor (execution).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (parking bay, void and kitchen position from the transcripts; ADB 5.6/5.7 and QCVN 06 3.2.6.2(a) read from the fetched documents). No separation detail exists in the set: VERIFIED as an absence. Whether a car is in fact parked there, and whether EV charging exists, is **NOT FOUND** — neither is stated anywhere.

### S-10

- **ID:** S-10
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, IMG_9972: *"Sân trước (-0.200, tag Ns, gate Dc) -> Nơi để xe (+-0.000, tag N1, door D1) 3940 internal / 4240 grid"*; IMG_9979 front elevation: *"D1 door 2800 x 2800 (READ; four-leaf glazed door with transom), 200 base below; stone cladding on both sides."*
- **Issue:** The front door D1 is a **4-leaf glazed door, 2800 × 2800 mm, with a 200 mm base below and a transom**, and it is the **final exit** for the whole house. A glazed final exit in a fire is a poor exit: it has no fire rating (or is rated only as glazing), it fails as a barrier, and it is the width over which smoke and heat pour into the house. There is no fire door anywhere on the escape route, and no vestibule.
- **Why it matters:** The final exit is the last point at which a fire is still outside. A fully glazed 2.8 m leaf is the worst possible choice for it: in a fire it either shatters (glass is a poor barrier to smoke and lets heat into the escape route) or holds and blocks escape. Either way the design has put the single most important door of the building in the worst possible material.
- **Basis:** UK ADB **para. 2.10** and Diagram 2.5 (escape windows/doors), and the general requirement of Requirement B1 that escape routes be *"adequately lit and exits … suitably signed"* and that fire not spread via the entrance. SCDF clause **2.2.9(b)** measures exit door clear width at 90° open, and clause **2.2.7(c)** caps a single leaf at 1250 mm — the four-leaf arrangement satisfies the width rule. QCVN 06 clause **3.4.7** requires the exit from the stairwell to discharge *"trực tiếp tới khu đất liền kề nhà"*.
- **Basis class:** BEYOND-LOCAL (ADB / SCDF practice on entrance doors) and JUDGEMENT (a glazed final exit in a single-stair house)
- **Fix:** Change the front door to a **solid-core or fire-rated door with a fire-rated glazed panel no larger than necessary**, at least one leaf of which is a self-closing fire door (E 20 minimum, EI 30 if it is also the stair enclosure door), or provide a protected entrance vestibule between the garage/yard and the living room. The transom over the door must be fixed (not openable) or fire-rated. Preserve the 2800 mm overall opening for ventilation and access, but do so with a door that can hold a barrier. Confirm the door does not swing onto the public street.
- **Who decides:** architect (detail), owner (aesthetics vs safety trade-off), fire consultant (rating).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (door description from the elevation transcript; clauses read from the fetched documents). Whether a fire rating was specified for the glazed leaves is **NOT FOUND** — no door schedule exists to carry it.

### S-11

- **ID:** S-11
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, IMG_9972 legend: *"N1 Ná»n trá»‡t (táº§ng 1) … = Ground floor, tiles"*; the finishes legend includes *"SL1 Sàn tầng lửng … = Mezzanine floor"*, *"SL2 … SL5 …"*, *"SST Sàn sân thượng"*, *"SM Sàn mái"*. There is **no** detection note on any sheet; `transcript-architecture.md`: *"the sheet has no circled 01..08 notes."*
- **Issue:** **There is no fire detection or alarm anywhere in the set.** No smoke alarm, no heat alarm, no call point, no control panel, no detector layout, no note of any kind on any of the 9 architectural sheets, and no MEP sheets at all. For a house with a double-height void, a car bay, a rear kitchen and a 5-storey single stair, this is the largest single omission in the safety scope.
- **Why it matters:** The early-warning system is the only fire-safety measure in a house that costs almost nothing and works when nothing else does. In a single-stair house, the occupants' chance of waking up is the difference between a controlled evacuation and a fatal one. ADB treats it as the first requirement: *"There are sufficient means for giving early warning of fire to people in the building."* A smoke alarm in the void and the stair, a heat alarm over the hob and in the garage bay, and interconnection across all floors is a few hundred thousand đồng.
- **Basis:** UK ADB **para. 1.1** — *"All dwellings should have a fire detection and alarm system, minimum Grade D2 Category LD3 standard, in accordance with the relevant recommendations of BS 5839-6."* ADB **para. 1.2–1.4** — *"Smoke alarms should be mains operated and conform to BS EN 14604. Heat alarms should be mains operated and conform to BS 5446-2. Smoke and heat alarms should have a standby power supply."* ADB **para. 1.7** — *"A large dwellinghouse of three or more storeys (excluding basement storeys) should be fitted with a Grade A Category LD2 fire detection and alarm system."* ADB **para. 1.9** — *"Smoke alarms should be provided in the circulation spaces of the dwelling in accordance with paragraphs 1.1 to 1.4."* ADB **para. 2.12(b)** — access rooms each need a smoke alarm. QCVN 06 clause **1.4.24** lists *"hệ thống báo cháy và âm thanh công cộng"* as a required component of the fire-protection system, and clause **3.2.6.2(a)3)** requires *"hệ thống báo cháy và chữa cháy tự động"* for a 21–25 m PCCC house with a single exit.
- **Basis class:** BEYOND-LOCAL (ADB Section 1) and LOCAL-MINIMUM (QCVN 06 3.2.6.2(a)3))
- **Fix:** Fit a **mains-operated, interconnected smoke-alarm system** (BS 5839-6 Grade D2 / Category LD3 minimum; recommend Grade A LD2 for a 6-storey house) with: smoke detectors in every circulation space and every habitable room; **heat detectors in the kitchen, in the garage bay and in the lift machine/plant room**; a detector in the double-height void at high level and at low level; detectors inside the stair enclosure at each level; a detector in the roof plant room; remote signalling or at minimum a sounder-and-relay unit that drives the fire-lift recall and any external alarm. Specify it on a drawing, not just in a schedule. Note also that a domestic system needs a **battery-backed supply** for every detector and an **emergency lighting / escape-signage** package on the escape route.
- **Who decides:** owner (budget and product), architect (layout and circuit), fire consultant (the specification), contractor (installation).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (absence of any detection note in the transcripts; ADB Section 1 and QCVN 06 clauses read from the fetched documents). Whether a system is to be installed is **NOT FOUND** — the set neither includes nor excludes one.

### S-12

- **ID:** S-12
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, "Common to all nine sheets": *"Legend vocabulary … Lan can kính cường lực dày 8 ly, gọn tay vịn inox = 8 mm tempered-glass balustrade with stainless-steel handrail."*; the same note is repeated on the mezzanine (*"the leader points at the rear edge line across the plan (on the rear 'Sân sau' opening, 3900 wide)"*), on floors 2, 3, 4 (front balcony and rear loggia) and on floor 5.
- **Issue:** **The party walls are entirely absent from the fire strategy.** The house is a 3.95 m-wide tube house with **shared walls on both sides**. The set contains no information about the party walls: no construction, no fire rating, no compartmentation line, no decision on whether the walls are shared, rebuilt or left as existing. Only a note about the neighbours' wall at the rear boundary ("2600 high element") appears.
- **Why it matters:** A party wall is not a boundary between two houses; it is *the* compartment wall, and it is the reason the neighbours are a fire-safety consideration at all. If the party walls are 100 mm brick (the section reads "200 | 350 repeatedly" for wall/column widths on the plan, but the party walls are drawn as 350 at the front and the walls in the section are unreported) they may have negligible fire resistance. And the failure mode is not abstract: a fire in the neighbouring house spreads through a common wall, and it reaches the owner's bedroom rather than the neighbour's.
- **Basis:** UK ADB **para. 5.5** — *"Dwellinghouses that are semi-detached or in terraces should be considered as separate buildings. Every wall separating the dwellinghouses should be constructed as a compartment wall (see paragraphs 5.8 to 5.12)."* ADB **para. 5.8** — *"All compartment walls and compartment floors should achieve both of the following. a. Form a complete barrier to fire between the compartments they separate. b. Have the appropriate fire resistance."* ADB **para. 5.10** — *"Compartment walls common to two or more buildings should comply with both of the following. a. Run the full height of the building in a continuous vertical plane. b. Be continued through any roof space to the underside of the roof."* ADB **para. 5.11** — a compartment wall must *"Meet the underside of the roof covering or deck, with fire-stopping to maintain the continuity of fire resistance"* and *"Be continued across any eaves."* ADB **para. 5.12** — *"To reduce the risk of fire spreading over the roof from one compartment to another, a 1500mm wide zone of the roof, either side of the wall, should have a covering classified as BROOF(t4)."* QCVN 06 clause **4.33.1** — vertical fire-spread barriers at the external wall; and clause **4.32** for internal separation, referencing *"dải ngăn cháy theo phương đứng, có bề rộng không nhỏ hơn 1 m"* at the external wall.
- **Basis class:** BEYOND-LOCAL (ADB 5.5–5.12) and LOCAL-MINIMUM (QCVN 06 4.32–4.33)
- **Fix:** Establish the party-wall strategy on the drawings: are the walls rebuilt or retained? If rebuilt, specify construction and a fire resistance appropriate to the bậc chịu lửa chosen (Table 4 of QCVN 06 for internal separation, ADB Table B3 for the English comparison), carried **continuously to the underside of the roof**, with fire-stopping at every slab penetration and a 1500 mm BROOF(t4) roof zone each side if the roof is rebuilt. If the walls are **retained existing** masonry, they need a survey and a fire-resistance opinion before anything else is fixed. Either way the owner must know whether the neighbours are behind a wall that will protect them or not, and the construction must be coordinated with the neighbours (S-28, S-29).
- **Who decides:** architect and licensed structural engineer (construction), fire consultant (rating), owner (negotiation with the neighbours), contractor (execution).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (absence of any party-wall note in the transcripts; ADB 5.5–5.12 and QCVN 06 4.32/4.33 read from the fetched documents). The construction of the existing party walls is **NOT FOUND** — it is not stated, and the structural sheet's *"TƯỜNG LÀM MỚI"* callout is itself ambiguous (new build, or the note referring only to the new building's own walls).

If open, it needs a 1200 mm guard at every level where it passes an opening (S-20) and must be excluded from the smoke path of the stair. Give the shaft a fire-resistance and smoke-sealing specification in the fire sheet (S-06).

- **Evidence label:** PARTIAL (light-well position, floor drains and the absent damper detail are VERIFIED from the transcripts, and the ADB/QCVN 06 clauses were read from the fetched documents; but whether the shaft is open or closed is ambiguous in the transcript's own wording, so the smoke-path conclusion is bounded by that).

### S-14

- **ID:** S-14
- **Severity:** MEDIUM
- **Where:** `transcript-architecture.md`, IMG_9979 front elevation: *"Ground floor: D1 door 2800 x 2800 … 200 base below; stone cladding on both sides"*; IMG_9972: *"Sân trước (-0.200 …) -> Nơi để xe (+-0.000 …)"*; the finishes legend: *"Ns Nền sân, lát gạch 30x60 cm, chống trượt = Yard floor, 30x60 non-slip tiles."*
- **Issue:** The **front yard is 200 mm below the parking-bay floor, and the parking-bay floor is at ±0.000 with a 200 mm base below the front door**. So the door threshold sits 200 mm above the yard. This is a trip and a wheelchair problem, and it is the only step between the public street and the final exit.
- **Why it matters:** Small but real: an evacuation from the front door into a 200 mm step in the dark, possibly with a burning ground floor behind, will cause falls. It also blocks step-free access (S-54).
- **Basis:** UK ADB **para. 3.107** — *"Final exits should not present a barrier for disabled people.
