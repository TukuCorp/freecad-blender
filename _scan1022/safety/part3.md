### S-23

- **ID:** S-23
- **Severity:** CRITICAL
- **Where:** `transcript-foundation-stairs.md`, IMG_9983 "MÓNG M1, M1a" GHI CHÚ note 4: *"ÉP CỌC BTCT 250x250 MÁC 300. CHIỀU DÀI CỌC LÀ 3x8=24m ( KỂ CẢ PHẦN ĐẬP ĐẦU CỌC LÀ 450mm VÀ PHẦN NGÀM VÀO MÓNG LÀ 150mm)."*; note 5: *"SỨC CHỊU TẢI CỦA CỌC LÀ Pcọc: 45T"*; note 6: *"TẢI KHI ÉP CỌC LÀ Pmax: 90T, Pmin: 67.5T"*. Same file: *"No soil / bearing data is stated on any of these sheets."*
- **Issue:** **There is no geotechnical report and no soil data of any kind in the set**, yet the design commits to 250 × 250 mm RC pressed piles, 24 m long, with a stated 45 t capacity and jacking loads of 67.5 t and 90 t. The 45 t capacity is stated as a flat figure with no derivation, no allowable settlement, no skin-friction assumption and no end-bearing depth. The site is described in the brief as soft Mekong-delta clay. The contract note 8 is the only concession: *"KHI THI CÔNG NẾU SỨC CHỊU TẢI CỦA CỌC KHÔNG ĐÚNG PHẢI BÁO THIẾT KẾ ĐỂ CÓ BIỆN PHÁP XỬ LÝ"* — that is, the design says "tell the designer if reality differs".
- **Why it matters:** Twenty-four-metre piles in soft delta clay for a 5-storey house is an extraordinary commitment, and it is the single most expensive and least reversible decision in the whole project. Piles are jacked into ground that is shared with two neighbours (S-28, S-29). If the assumed shaft friction or the assumed 45 t capacity is wrong, the options are: more piles (no room — the plot is 3.9 m wide), longer piles (unlikely to help in a soft clay layer of that depth), or a raft/micropile redesign (a complete re-do of the foundation set). The **settlement consequence for the neighbours** is the scenario that actually hurts people: differential settlement of a party wall causes cracking in the neighbour's house, and on a 24 m pile in soft clay, differential settlement is the expected behaviour, not the exceptional one.
- **Basis:** This is an engineering-judgement screening item, not a clause. The published standard practice is that pile capacity must be derived from a site-specific geotechnical investigation and checked against a stated settlement criterion; **TCVN 2737:2023 (design of foundations) and the building-foundation standard were NOT FOUND in this review** (see §1.3), so no clause is quoted. What *is* quotable is the drawing's own admission that there is no soil data. The requirement for a licensed structural engineer with a geotechnical basis is the professional norm in every code consulted (QCVN 06 requires the structural design to be carried out to a standard; the standards themselves were not retrievable).
- **Basis class:** JUDGEMENT
- **Fix:** **Do not accept the foundation set for construction until a geotechnical investigation report exists** — minimum: 2–3 CPTs (cone penetration) or boreholes to at least 30 m depth along the plot, with continuous sampling and SPT in the clay, and a laboratory soil report giving undrained shear strength profiles. The report must state: the derived pile capacity method, the design pile length, the number of piles and caps, the expected and differential settlement of the pile group, the negative skin friction / consolidation settlement over the building's life, and any ground-improvement requirement. Then have the pile design (capacity, length, jacking pressure, and the pile cap) re-issued by a licensed structural engineer against that data, with the engineer's stamp and licence number on every structural sheet. The current sheet has neither (S-06).
- **Who decides:** owner (pays for the investigation — a few tens of millions of đồng, against a foundation bill an order of magnitude larger), licensed structural engineer (the investigation scope and the design), architect (coordinates the pit and pile layout with the neighbours).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (the pile note and the absence of soil data are quoted from the transcript). The governing standards were **NOT FOUND** and no clause is invented, so the finding rests on the drawing's own admission that there is no soil data.

### S-28

- **ID:** S-28
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Where:** `transcript-foundation-stairs.md`, IMG_9983 GHI CHÚ notes 4–7: *"ÉP CỌC BTCT 250x250 MÁC 300. CHIỀU DÀI CỌC LÀ 3x8=24m … SỨC CHỊU TẢI CỦA CỌC LÀ Pcọc: 45T … TẢI KHI ÉP CỌC LÀ Pmax: 90T, Pmin: 67.5T … ĐẦU CỌC CÓ HÀN MIẾNG THÉP BẢO VỆ"*.
- **Issue:** **Jacking 24 m piles at 67.5–90 t into soft delta clay, on a plot with a party wall on each side, is a vibration and a displacement problem, not just a load problem.** The piles are 250 × 250 mm — a small section for a 24 m pile, which means a long slender element — and the jacking loads are high. Nothing in the set addresses vibration limits, monitoring, or the condition of the neighbours.
- **Why it matters:** Pile jacking in soft clay displaces and heaves the ground; the adjacent ground surface rises and the adjacent building's foundations heave and settle differentially. On a shared wall with a neighbouring house of unknown foundation type, this is how a neighbour's house gets damaged — and it happens long before anyone notices, because the damage shows up as new cracks in a party wall months later. The neighbours are also likely to be on their own piles or on shallow footings in the same soft clay, so the response is not predictable from this side. A 90 t jacking load against a 250 mm pile at 24 m depth in clay also has a real buckling risk during jacking, and the head protection plate noted in the drawing (*"hàn miếng thép bảo vệ"*) addresses damage to the pile, not the ground.
- **Basis:** JUDGEMENT (no retrieved code addresses pile-jacking vibration limits for this condition). What is verifiable from the set is the jacking load, the pile section and the length, and the complete absence of any vibration or monitoring provision.
- **Basis class:** JUDGEMENT
- **Fix:** Before jacking: (i) a **pre-construction condition survey of both neighbours** (S-29) with dated, high-resolution photographs of every crack, and a written record agreed with both owners; (ii) **continuous vibration monitoring** during jacking with a defined alert and stop threshold agreed with the neighbours and the structural engineer; (iii) **settlement/level monitoring** of the party walls and the neighbour's floors using tell-tales or a survey; (iv) a jacking sequence that avoids, where possible, jacking immediately against the party walls — the layout puts piles on both sides, so the sequence should work from the centre out, or the party-wall piles should be jacked first with monitoring; (v) a written protocol for what happens if a threshold is exceeded. Note the jacking load must be revisited against the geotechnical report (S-23), because 45 t per pile on a 250 × 250 section is a jacking-load-driven design, not a capacity-driven one.
- **Who decides:** licensed structural engineer (the jacking plan), contractor (execution and monitoring), owner (neighbour liaison and the monitoring contract), neighbours (informed consent where possible).
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (pile section, length, capacity and jacking loads quoted from the transcript; the absence of vibration/monitoring provisions verified as an absence). The neighbours' foundation types are **NOT FOUND**.

### S-43 — seismic detailing, wind and soft soil

- **ID:** S-43
- **Severity:** MEDIUM
- **Where:** The whole set. `transcript-foundation-stairs.md`: *"No soil / bearing data is stated on any of these sheets."*; `transcript-beams-slabs.md`: the only seismic-adjacent content is the stirrup detailing (*"Ø6a:100-200"*, *"Ø6a (a100÷200)"*) and the note *"CHỖ NÀO CÓ TƯỜNG MÀ KHÔNG CÓ BỐ TRÍ DẦM THÌ TA ĐẶT THÊM 2Ø16 HOẶC 3Ø16"*. No bracing, no ductility detailing, no anchorage lengths, no lap lengths.
- **Issue:** HCMC is in a low-to-moderate seismicity zone, but this is a **6-storey, 3.95 m wide, 25 m deep building on soft delta clay with a 45 t pile per pile and a house of 200 mm columns**. The set contains: no seismic design category, no ductility class, no confinement detailing beyond Ø6 ties at 100/200, **no anchorage or lap lengths anywhere**, and no statement of whether the structure is intended as a moment frame, a shear-wall system or a flat slab. On soft soil, the two governing actions are not the earthquake but **wind** (a 25 m deep, slender, 21.9 m tall building with an open terrace and a plant room at the top, catching typhoon-season wind across its narrow face and vortex shedding along its length) and **ground movement** (differential settlement and lateral ground strain from adjacent loading and from the neighbours' own foundations).
- **Why it matters:** The screen is not "is it safe in an earthquake" — it is that **nothing in the set states the structural system, so nothing can be checked.** A 6-storey building on 200 mm columns with Ø6 ties and no anchorage lengths is either a well-detailed frame (in which case the drawings must say so, with the anchorage and lap lengths) or an under-detailed one (in which case it is a serious problem). The drawings do not allow the distinction to be made.
- **Basis:** JUDGEMENT. **TCVN 9386:2012 (seismic analysis and design) and TCVN 2737:2023 were NOT FOUND** (§1.3), so no clause is quoted. What is verifiable is the complete absence of any ductility, anchorage or bracing information, and the column/slenderness data in S-31.
- **Basis class:** JUDGEMENT
- **Fix:** The licensed structural engineer must state on the drawings: the structural system, the seismic design parameters used and the site class, the ductility class, the design category for wind (including the vortex-shedding check for a 25 m slender plan), and the **full anchorage and lap schedule** (currently entirely absent — the unexplained "400" dimensions on the ĐK sheets are not a schedule). If the design is a ductile frame, the confinement at the beam-column joints and the anchorage development must be shown in detail; if it is a shear-wall or wall-frame system, the walls must appear on the plans and be founded accordingly — and note that no wall appears in the foundation layout other than the grade beams, which is itself a question for the engineer.
- **Who decides:** licensed structural engineer.
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (the absences and the stirrup data quoted from the transcripts). TCVN 9386 is **NOT FOUND**.

### S-44 — robustness against progressive collapse

- **ID:** S-44
- **Severity:** MEDIUM
- **Where:** `transcript-foundation-stairs.md`, IMG_9985: *"M4 is a single-pile cap (6Ø20 column, C3). A single-pile support has no redundancy and no tie-beam check is stated."*; IMG_9986: *"All M6/M7/M8 caps are single-pile caps with an eccentric column"*.
- **Issue:** **Seven columns are founded on single piles with no redundancy**, the structural system is undeclared (S-43), and there is no tie-beam design (S-25) — so the tying action that would provide the alternative load path is not documented.
- **Why it matters:** Progressive collapse is the question "if one thing fails, does the building still stand?". Here the answer is unknown, and for seven of the columns the honest answer is "each stands on exactly one 250 mm square concrete element in soft clay". That is not automatically unacceptable — a properly tied pile cap with a designed grade beam can carry the neighbouring pile if one settles or fails — but the drawing set does not show it. This is a robustness question the owner should put to the engineer, framed as "show the load path if one pile is lost", not as an assertion that it fails.
- **Basis:** JUDGEMENT.
- **Basis class:** JUDGEMENT
- **Fix:** Ask the engineer to demonstrate, by calculation and by detail, the load path for the loss of (a) a single pile under a two-pile cap, and (b) the pile under a single-pile cap — and to show the tie-beam (ĐG) design that provides it. If the answer is that the tie beams are the mechanism, then the ĐG sections (currently absent from the layout sheet, S-25) and their connections become a priority issue.
- **Who decides:** licensed structural engineer.
- **Evidence label:** VERIFIED (single-pile caps quoted from the transcript).
